The Magpie's Hoard

A magpie has stolen a diamond off a window ledge, and it is somewhere in the nest. The trouble is, the nest is forty years of everything shiny that ever got left out: bottle tops, a teaspoon, a bike light, sequins, a chandelier drop, a frog that wandered in. Twenty-five things, and it is dark in there, and every one of them is doing its best to look like a diamond.

You are going in. You have a torch, which is your finger, and you have two questions for everything you find. What is the light doing? And what is the thing made of? Get both right and you know whether to pocket it or drop it. Get the first right and the second wrong and a glass bead goes home in your pocket. People have been fooled by glass beads for about five thousand years. Don't be one of them.

“All that glisters is not gold.”William Shakespeare, The Merchant of Venice. Not glitters. He wrote glisters. Everyone has been misquoting him for four hundred years.
